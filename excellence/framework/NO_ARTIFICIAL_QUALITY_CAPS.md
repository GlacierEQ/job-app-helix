# No artificial quality caps

**Captured:** 2026-08-09T05:45Z  
**Source:** operator preference (Casey / GlacierEQ)

## Law

Do **not** enforce “flagship-only depth” or deliberately leave packs at C/D  
“for later” as a standing strategy.

That pacing made sense when the surface was still a **2D presentation**  
(thin portfolio pages, single-layer story). The estate is **not 2D** anymore:

- multi-repo packs per company  
- Babel W4H multi-language companions  
- atlas / machine / master layers  
- harness composition + claim ceilings  

## How we work instead

1. **Same rigor structure for every admitted pack** — contracts, refuse paths, tests, W4H language placement, honesty docs.  
2. **Queue is logistics, not a quality ceiling** — order of work ≠ allowed depth.  
3. **Babel always** — language follows problem physics; no decorative polyglot, no Python-only by default when Go/C/TS/SQL earns the slot.  
4. **No overclaim** — depth ≠ employer affiliation or REPRODUCED without receipts.  
5. **If something is thin, treat it as incomplete work**, not as an intentional tier.

## Anti-pattern (banned)

> “We’ll keep the long tail at C+ on purpose so the top 3 look special.”

That is presentation logic. This is a multi-dimensional systems estate.

## Compatible with

- Prioritizing *when* to push/redeploy (device, time)  
- Explicit L1/L2 *depth campaigns* as **expansion**, never as caps on other packs
